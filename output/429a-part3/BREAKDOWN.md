# 429A — Part 3: "It's Leo"

## 1. Concept
Part 2 ended with Leo locked inside room 429A (still 1986 inside), the handle gone, and three knocks. Viewers were offered A (peephole) or B (ledge); A was chosen.

Part 2's "his name is in the guest book" twist was a familiar horror beat, so Part 3 had to reframe the whole story rather than add another scare. The twist ties off all three loose ends from Parts 1–2 at once:

- **Who is knocking?** Leo from earlier tonight.
- **Who called from 429A at 4.29?** Leo. The room phone is off the hook and connected to the front desk.
- **Who wrote "Rule 1" in Leo's handwriting?** Leo. The note on the desk is half written and the ink is still wet.

It is a time loop he has been through "hundreds of times". The cliffhanger is that M. Hale's 1986 signature is also Leo's handwriting. That opens Part 4 without explaining it.

## 2. Title
There is no room 429A. Part 3. And the person knocking is Leo.

## 3. Full narration
1. Leo is locked inside room 429A. Someone is knocking. … He looks through the peephole. … And the person knocking. … Is Leo.
2. Same uniform. Same name tag. … It's Leo, from earlier tonight. … The moment he first walked up to this door.
3. Behind him, the phone by the bed is off the hook. … He lifts it. … Through the line, he can hear a phone ringing. … The front desk phone. Downstairs.
4. The call from room 429A. … It's coming from him. … It always was.
5. Like and follow, if your brain hurts too.
6. On the desk, there's a pad of sticky notes. … The top one is half written. … Rule one. Never go up to a room that doesn't. … The ink is still wet.
7. Leo wrote the warning. … He's been in this room before. … Maybe hundreds of times.
8. Then he checks the guest book again. … M. Hale. 1986. … The same handwriting. … Leo's.
9. Option A. Finish the note, and slide it under the door. Option B. Pick up the phone, and warn himself. Comment A or B. And tell me what the note should say.

## 5. Visual prompts
- **Style (all scenes):** realistic 3D, PS4-era quality. Warm tungsten light, ACES tone mapping, soft shadows, film grain, slight sepia grade, vignette. 9:16.
- **Room 429A (1986):**
  - orange shag carpet and brown and orange circle-pattern wallpaper
  - wood wainscot and a bed with a rust-and-mustard floral bedspread
  - cream rotary phone on a wooden bedside table with an orange lamp
  - huge wood-cabinet CRT showing static, and a record player with a spinning red-label record
  - writing desk with a green banker lamp, the guest book and a yellow sticky-note pad
  - mustard armchair, a tea cup with steam, and a window onto a night city
- **Leo:** young night porter. Burgundy porter jacket, brass name tag, dark trousers, short brown hair. Same model in every shot.
- **Peephole view:** circular fisheye vignette with a brass ring and a black surround. The corridor has green striped wallpaper and brass door plates. First a silhouette in near darkness, then lit.
- **Flashback corridor:** the same hotel corridor, sepia-tinted, seen from behind Leo walking toward 429A between 429 and 430.
- **Lobby (Part 1 set):** marble reception desk, black desk phone with a green LCD reading 429A, a wall clock at 4.29 and a key cabinet with a red 429A tag.
- **Sticky notes:** yellow, handwritten in blue italic ink. Hundreds of them overlap on the inside of the door.
- **Split screen:** a cream guest-book card reading "M. Hale" in sepia italic, above a yellow note reading "NEVER go up…" in blue italic.

## 6. Editing instructions
- **Hook:** say it, show it, write it. "WHO IS KNOCKING?" is on screen from frame 1. The reveal lands at 7.7s with a light flick, a white flash, a bass hit and a sting.
- **Pacing:** a visual change every 1.5–3s. Longer pauses go before "Is Leo", "It always was", "The ink is still wet" and "Leo's".
- **Transitions:** a zoom-blur punch on cuts. A sepia tint plus tape slow-down for the flashback. A J-cut on the phone line (the ring is heard before the lobby is shown).
- **SFX (actions only):**
  - knocks, the lens creak and the light flick
  - handset click, line hiss, a muffled ring and then the full ring
  - a digital glitch on the clock
  - paper tear, a paper flurry for ×347, and a heartbeat into the handwriting hit
  - choice ticks
- **Music:** low hotel drone. It drops to near silence at the reveal and returns with the case file.
- **Captions:** word-synced, max 5 words. On-screen text uses "." instead of ":" and "-".
- **HUD:**
  - case file: knocker, call, Rule 1, handwriting
  - clock glitch: 4.47 jumps back to 4.29
  - PART 3 chip and like/follow pill
- **Ending:** loops back to frame 1.

## 7. Final quality assessment

**Strengths**
- The opening has a concrete question on screen from frame 1 and a fast, visual answer. It works for new viewers too.
- The twist pays off Part 1's mysteries instead of piling on new ones. That rewards people who followed, which is the point of a series.
- The comment ask, "what should the note say?", invites real answers rather than single letters. That should help the comment rate, which was lower on earlier videos.

**Weaknesses**
- The snapback lands at about 7.7s. Ideally it would land by about 3s, so the 6–9s drop seen on earlier graphs could still happen here.
- 65s is on the long side. The flashback (scene 6) and the "security cam" shot (scene 12) are the most cuttable.
- Both Leos use the same simple character model. The reveal relies on the uniform and name tag rather than facial detail.
- Time loops are a known trope. What keeps it fresh is the physical evidence: the wet ink, the ×347 notes and the matching signature. Part 4 must explain M. Hale without hand-waving, or the series loses trust.
